About

The interesting part of hardware isn't the schematic. It's everything that happens after, when a design has to survive being manufactured, soldered, and switched on.

I'm an Electrical & Electronic Engineering graduate with hands-on time across ESP32 and RP2040 platforms, multi-layer PCB layout, and firmware debugging in C. During an internship at Sri Lanka Telecom I carried an early-stage prototype through to a field-deployed, mass-production-ready device. That kind of process teaches you how much of hardware engineering is triage, not design.

Since February 2026 I've worked in product engineering as a contract hardware design engineer for international clients: RF and power subsystems around Nordic nRF52-series MCUs with front-end module integration, a Qi wireless charging receiver, and fabrication-ready schematics and PCB routing.

My final year project, JumperZ Studio, lives at that same intersection: a programmable breadboard built around a custom RP2040/ESP32 mainboard, driving a 12× CH446Q switching matrix across 400 addressable LEDs, controlled entirely from a ~150-module desktop application I designed and built from scratch in Python.

Outside the FYP I've laid out complete boards in EasyEDA and led the mechanical and sensor design across two competition Micromouse robots, built with a 5-person team over two years. Firmware is where I'm investing time now. The hardware side is solid, and getting the chips to do exactly what I intend is the next chapter.
